# Methodology and defensible interpretation

## State and condition

Each of eight components has a permanent ID, type, age, current condition H ∈ [0,100], cumulative loading, material factor, maintenance interval, protection, condition category, and source. The deck has weight 3; each pier and the bearing group weight 1; each joint weight 0.5. These are configured importance assumptions. They sum to 9.

Bridge condition = Σ(wᵢHᵢ) / Σwᵢ. The risk index is Σ[wᵢ(100−Hᵢ)²/100] / Σwᵢ. The latter emphasizes poor-condition components and is **not** a probability of failure. Health category thresholds are controlled by `RISK_THRESHOLDS`. Dashboard priority uses the worst component as well as counts below 60.

## Monthly equation

The annual loss rate is a product of transparent factors:

`r = base × age_factor × loading_factor × exposure_factor × delay_factor × fatigue_factor × material_factor × protection_factor`

| Factor | Exact implementation |
|---|---|
| Base loss, condition points/year | deck 0.65; pier 0.55; bearing 0.9; joint 0.85 |
| Age | 1 + 0.018 × component_age |
| Loading | 1 + 0.65 × (traffic/100)^1.4 + 1.1 × (heavy_vehicle_percent/100)^1.6 |
| Exposure | 1 + 0.45 × exposure + 0.12 × rainfall/2000 + 0.1 × abs(temperature−22)/25 |
| Delayed care | 1 + 0.055 × max(0, years_since_maintenance−4) + 0.09 × maintenance_delay |
| Fatigue proxy | 1 + 0.008 × cumulative_load |
| Material | Fixed component susceptibility factor |
| Protection | 1 − 0.4 × protection |

For each monthly increment Δt=1/12 year, `H_next=max(0,H−rΔt)`. Age and maintenance interval increase by Δt. `cumulative_load += (traffic/100)(1+2×heavy_vehicle_percent/100)Δt`. Protection declines by 0.08Δt toward zero. Cumulative load is a normalized exposure-years proxy, **not tonnes, axle loads, strain, or engineering stress**.

Synthetic histories add seeded rate noise with standard deviation 0.06; operational simulation does not inject independent random scores. Sinusoidal seasonal variations evolve traffic, heavy vehicles, temperature and rainfall coherently. A future scenario keeps chosen base operating assumptions constant for its forecast. Therefore a stepped seasonal simulation and a constant-input forecast need not coincide exactly.

These functional relationships are plausible modeling choices, not published empirical coefficients. They are deliberately documented so they can be replaced through calibration. No engineering law or standard is claimed as the source of these numbers.

## Synthetic data

Default seed 42; 160 bridges; eight components per bridge; twelve annual transitions each. Bridge age, operating environment and material susceptibility are sampled, then health is generated by integration from a commissioning condition of 100. Ten named scenario families change relevant inputs together. Recovery interventions create jumps only where explicitly configured. The bundled fictional bridge is generated by the same dynamics over 18 years with recorded synthetic maintenance.

This creates a controlled software experiment. Because the generator and the physics predictor share equations, the physics baseline is expected to perform strongly. It cannot demonstrate predictive validity for real bridges. Neither favorable metrics nor a polished visual twin change that limitation.

## Training and forecasting

Independent bridge IDs are split into approximately 60% training, 15% validation, 10% calibration, and 15% test. Whole trajectories stay in one split, preventing repeated measurements from the same bridge leaking across subsets. Actual counts and bridge lists are stored in the model card. This follows the group-disjoint split mechanism documented in [scikit-learn GroupShuffleSplit](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.model_selection.GroupShuffleSplit.html).

RF and Gradient Boosting learn the annual loss `previous_health−target_health_1y`. Prediction reconstructs health by subtracting the estimated loss, clipped to `[0, previous_health]`. The best validation MAE selects the estimator. It is not refitted on calibration or test data. The final inference uses a configured 65/35 physics/model blend and advances age/load/maintenance features annually, recursively through the requested horizon.

All reported MAE, RMSE and R² values are calculated from held-out rows, with both raw ML and hybrid scores reported. Persistence and physics baselines are included. When real data is explicitly supplied, validation/calibration/test splits contain only real bridge groups; synthetic rows augment training only. Minimum real bridge count is 20, a workflow guard rather than evidence of statistical adequacy.

## Uncertainty and explainability

Absolute hybrid residuals on calibration data determine an upper finite-sample 90th percentile. The one-year reference band is prediction ± that value, clipped to [0,100]. Test coverage is calculated and reported. Rows within bridges remain dependent; the band is not guaranteed to have nominal coverage. It excludes structural-model error and synthetic-to-real domain shift. For multiple years, width scales with √horizon; this is a transparent illustrative approximation, not a validated confidence interval. Bridge-average bands use the same residual scale conservatively as a reference, without asserting aggregate calibration.

Two computed explanation views are provided: normalized model-wide impurity importance for learned deterioration; and one-input sensitivity that replaces an operating variable with its training median and reports the change in the blended one-year health estimate. Sensitivities do not add up to a prediction, do not measure causality, and can combine unrealistic reference inputs. A feature-range warning flags extrapolation.

Isolation Forest is fitted only on training features, with contamination 3%. Negative decision score = Anomalous; 0 to 0.03 = Warning; above = Normal. These thresholds refer to input distribution, not structural damage.

## Maintenance and optimization

The action catalog contains configured INR cost, condition gain, temporary protection, duration and priority. A repair increases health only to the configured recovery cap of 98, resets the maintenance interval, and adds decaying protection. No state improves merely because an inspection is selected.

For action j, benefit = wᵢ × [((100−Hᵢ)²−(100−Hᵢ−gainⱼ)²)/100]. Binary variable xⱼ chooses the action. Maximize Σ benefitⱼxⱼ subject to Σ costⱼxⱼ≤budget and Σ actions_for_component xⱼ≤1. This uses [SciPy `milp`](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.optimize.milp.html) with HiGHS, requiring proven solver success. An empty plan is always feasible for nonnegative budget.

The optimized objective is immediate condition-risk reduction. Forward maintenance trajectories separately show the future effect. Duration is informational; concurrent work, scheduling, repair interactions, mobilization, inspection value-of-information and contractor feasibility are not modeled.

## Interpretation to use in a jury

“This project demonstrates an end-to-end simulation-driven digital twin architecture. Its forecasts are reproducible and data-driven, but the current models are trained and evaluated on synthetic scenarios. We validate software behavior and assumptions, not real bridge safety. Real inspection data and structural-engineering calibration are necessary before any operational use.”
